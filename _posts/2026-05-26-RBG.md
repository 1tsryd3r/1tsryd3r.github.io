> RBG stands for RSA + SBG + LCG!

## Analysis

- We are given 13 outputs of an RSA encryption where the exponent is derived from an LCG, and has a small modulus $N$ of 274 bits.
- The LCG for the exponent can be represented as $e_{next} \equiv 3e_{curr} + 1337 \pmod N$
- The modulo $N$ is important here, making the equation as $e_{next} = 3e_{curr} + 1337 - kN$.
- Initially, I did not think of the $-kN$ factor, calculating $\frac{c_{next}}{c_{curr}^3} \equiv m^{1337} \pmod N$, to find $m^{1337}$. The modulus $N$ is factorable on `factordb`, and hence $\phi$ and $d$ can be calculated. However, this approach won't work due to the modulo $N$ in the exponent LCG, making this a hard-level challenge.
- Now considering the modulo $N$, we get $c_{next} \equiv c_{curr}^3 \cdot m^{1337} \cdot m^{-kN} \pmod N$. Consider the constant terms as $A \equiv m^{1337} \pmod N$ and $B \equiv m^N \pmod N$.
- To find $A$ and $B$, let $x \equiv \frac{c_{next}}{c_{curr}^3} \pmod N$, which simplifies to $x \equiv A \cdot B^{-k} \pmod N$.
- We can then cycle through all possible pairs of the ciphertexts to find a pair such that $k_2 - k_1 = 1$. When this occurs, $\frac{x_1}{x_2} \equiv B \pmod N$.
- Once we have $B$, we can plug it back into $x \equiv A \cdot B^{-k} \pmod N$, bruting for the small values of $k$ to find $A \equiv x \cdot B^k \pmod N$.
- Now that we have $A \equiv m^{1337} \pmod N$ and $B \equiv m^N \pmod N$, we use the Extended Euclidean Algorithm on 1337 and $N$ to find the Bezout coefficients $a$ and $b$ such that $1337a + Nb = 1$.
- By doing so, we can reconstruct the message as $m \equiv A^a \cdot B^b \pmod N$, which gives us the flag.
